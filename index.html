How it runs

  1. Solar first. The array supplies the loads. Any extra charges the batteries.
  2. Batteries fill the gap. At night, on cloudy days or under heavy use, the batteries cover what solar can't.
  3. Generator on low battery. When the battery falls to the start point, the inverter starts the generator through a small relay.
  4. Generator charges, then stops. The generator carries the loads, puts the rest of its output into the batteries, and shuts off at the stop point.

The generator starts because the battery is low, not because something big switched on. The bottom 10% of the battery is held in reserve, which leaves 36.9 kWh to use. The start and stop points are inverter settings; the simulator uses 25% and 90%. If the inverter ever fails, a manual bypass switch lets the generator run the camp directly.

Panel wiring

The 36 panels are wired in four groups ("strings") of 9. Phase 1 installs three strings (75%); phase 2 adds the fourth (25%). The owner asked for two-thirds, then one-third, but string length is fixed by voltage, so the split front-loads capacity for summer A/C.

Solar input (MPPT)Limits (Sol-Ark 15K)StringsIsc / ImpPhase
  • String voltage: 296V at STC maximum power and about 251–256V on a hot day, inside Sol-Ark's 150–425V MPPT range.
  • Cold open-circuit voltage: 408V at the state record low of −37°F (−0.24%/K), under the 500V maximum.
  • MPPT 1 clips negligibly after phase 2 (26.1A against 26A).
